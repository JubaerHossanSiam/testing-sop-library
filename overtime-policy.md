# Overtime Policy

## Purpose

Define when overtime is permitted, how it is approved and recorded, and how approved overtime is compensated.

Normal working hours are defined in [Office Hours Policy](./office-hours-policy.md). This policy applies only to approved work beyond the applicable normal schedule.

## Core Principle

Blinto does **not encourage routine overtime**. Overtime is an exception, not the normal operating model.

Managers should plan capacity, scope, staffing, and deadlines so recurring overtime is not used as a substitute for good planning.

There is no arbitrary evening threshold—such as 7:00 PM—that must be reached before work can qualify as overtime. Overtime is based on approved work beyond the applicable normal schedule and the applicable employment/legal treatment.

## When Overtime May Be Justified

Examples include:

### International or After-Hours Client Commitments

- unavoidable US or other international client meetings;
- Right Left Agency (RLA) meetings that must occur outside Blinto's normal office hours;
- client workshops or strategy sessions that cannot reasonably be scheduled within normal hours.

### Critical Support or Incident Work

- production outage or website downtime;
- serious security incident;
- emergency bug fix;
- critical deployment with a genuine time constraint; or
- another incident where immediate work is reasonably necessary to protect service, security, client interests, or company assets.

### Deadline-Critical Delivery

- an urgent client requirement or business-critical delivery that cannot reasonably be rescheduled and has been approved as requiring overtime.

A deadline alone does not automatically justify overtime when the underlying issue is avoidable poor planning or unmanaged scope.

## Overtime Is Not Justified By

Overtime should not be approved merely because of:

- late arrival;
- poor routine productivity;
- routine unfinished work;
- avoidable poor planning;
- avoidable rework or mistakes;
- failure to manage scope or capacity early enough; or
- a personal scheduling conflict that does not create an approved business need.

Where these issues recur, the responsible lead should address the underlying planning, performance, scope, or attendance problem rather than normalize overtime.

## Approval

### Planned Overtime

Planned overtime requires:

**Team/Department Lead → People Operations/HR → CEO**

The request should identify:

- why overtime is necessary;
- the employee/team member(s) required;
- the project/client where applicable;
- the expected start/end time or duration where practical; and
- the expected work or outcome.

No planned overtime should begin until the required approval is obtained.

### Emergency Overtime

Where an immediate incident makes advance approval impractical, the responsible lead may authorize the minimum emergency work necessary to protect service, security, client interests, or company assets.

The overtime must then be documented and escalated to People Operations/HR and the CEO as soon as practical, with the record completed no later than the next practical business-day administration cycle.

## Compensation

Blinto compensates approved overtime rather than treating it as unpaid extra work.

For employees/workers covered by applicable Bangladesh statutory overtime provisions, approved overtime is paid at **2× the applicable ordinary overtime wage basis**, using the legally applicable wage components and calculation method.

For employees whose role/classification is not covered by that statutory calculation, Blinto's internal default is **2× the employee's approved hourly base rate** for approved overtime unless the employment contract or another approved policy provides a more favorable treatment.

People Operations/HR must confirm the applicable classification and payroll calculation before payment. Managers should not independently promise or calculate a different multiplier.

## Recording & Payroll

Approved overtime hours must be recorded accurately through the current attendance/payroll process.

The record should identify, where applicable:

- employee/team member name or ID;
- date;
- project;
- client;
- overtime start time;
- overtime end time;
- total approved overtime hours;
- reason;
- work completed or outcome;
- approving Team/Department Lead;
- People Operations/HR verification;
- CEO approval or emergency escalation status; and
- payroll/compensation treatment.

Unrecorded or unapproved overtime should be investigated rather than automatically normalized as a recurring work practice.

## Manager Responsibilities

Leads/managers should:

- avoid creating a culture where staying late signals higher commitment;
- approve/request overtime only when justified;
- use the minimum reasonable overtime required;
- ensure overtime is recorded and escalated correctly; and
- treat repeated overtime as a capacity, planning, scope, staffing, or process signal.

## People Operations Responsibilities

People Operations/HR:

- reviews planned overtime requests;
- maintains required overtime records;
- confirms classification and compensation calculation;
- coordinates payroll treatment;
- monitors recurring overtime patterns; and
- escalates systemic overtime issues to leadership.

## Future Policy Options

Blinto may later adopt additional overtime controls without reducing statutory or contractual entitlements, including:

- compensatory time off for eligible roles where legally and contractually appropriate;
- defined monthly overtime caps;
- stricter approval thresholds for non-emergency overtime;
- team-level capacity review when overtime repeats;
- time-window restrictions for non-critical work;
- mandatory recovery/rest after unusually late critical work; and
- separate treatment for client meetings outside normal hours versus execution work.

These are **not current rules** unless formally approved and incorporated into the canonical policy.

## Exceptions & Escalation

Escalate to People Operations/HR when:

- overtime is becoming routine for a person/team;
- approval or compensation treatment is unclear;
- classification/payroll calculation is uncertain;
- emergency work occurred before normal approval;
- a manager/team member disputes recorded overtime; or
- a contractual or legal requirement may provide a more favorable entitlement.
