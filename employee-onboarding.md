# Employee Onboarding

## Purpose

Provide a consistent process for preparing a new team member, introducing the company and role, provisioning appropriate access, and establishing a clear first-week path.

Use the [Employee Onboarding Checklist](./employee-onboarding-checklist.md) to execute and verify each onboarding. This SOP owns the process; the checklist should not redefine it.

## When to Use

Use after an approved candidate has accepted the applicable employment/internship engagement and before they begin normal work.

## Owner

People Operations coordinates onboarding. The team/department lead owns role-specific onboarding, work expectations, and the initial work/learning plan.

## Preconditions

Before onboarding begins, confirm:

- accepted offer/appointment/engagement documentation;
- agreed start date;
- role and reporting relationship;
- responsible lead/manager;
- required systems/tools/access;
- equipment/workspace requirements where applicable.

## Procedure

### 1. Create the onboarding plan

People Operations coordinates the onboarding timeline and identifies who is responsible for each preparation item.

Do not provision unnecessary privileged access simply because previous employees in a similar role had it.

### 2. Prepare required records and equipment

Prepare applicable employment/HR records, workspace, equipment, and required administrative documentation before the start date where practical.

Sensitive personal/employment information must remain in the appropriate controlled system/location.

### 3. Provision identity and tool access

Provision only the tools required for the role. Depending on the role, this may include company email/identity, ClickUp, Google Drive/Workspace resources, approved communication tools, approved password manager, and department/project systems.

Use least privilege. Privileged/client access should be granted only when required and through the applicable access/security process.

### 4. Send pre-start communication

Provide the new joiner with the information needed to arrive/start successfully, including start date/time, location or joining instructions, point of contact, and any required pre-start actions.

### 5. Conduct company orientation

On or near Day One, People Operations covers company context, relevant culture/values, employment basics, key policies, working expectations, security/confidentiality, communication/escalation channels, and the SOP Library.

Do not reproduce entire policies during orientation; link/provide the canonical policy resources.

### 6. Conduct team/role orientation

The responsible lead/manager introduces the team and collaborators, explains role outcomes, confirms reporting/communication expectations, introduces relevant projects/clients, identifies required SOPs/resources, assigns the initial work/learning plan, and identifies a mentor/buddy where required.

### 7. Complete first-week setup

Verify that the new joiner can access required tools and understands where to find work, documentation, support, and escalation paths. Provide initial tasks appropriate to role/readiness.

### 8. Establish review/check-in expectations

Explain how early performance/learning feedback will work, including probation/internship check-ins where applicable. Use [Performance Review](./performance-review.md) for formal/recurring evaluation.

### 9. Close onboarding

Complete and retain the applicable [Employee Onboarding Checklist](./employee-onboarding-checklist.md). Onboarding is complete when administrative setup, required access/equipment, role clarity, immediate priorities, policy/security orientation, initial work/learning plan, and check-in expectations are complete, with any outstanding issue assigned to an owner.

## Exceptions / Escalation

Escalate when approved role/terms differ from onboarding instructions, critical access/equipment will not be ready, requested access appears excessive, required documentation is incomplete, or responsibility is unclear.
